**Author**: Marlon James

**Format**: Paperback (technically an advance reader copy I guess)

_Black Leopard, Red Wolf_ is a fantasy novel set in an Africa-inspired world that melds what seems to be a wide range of sub-Saharan myths / folklore / etc together - there are direct references to e.g. Nan Si, Simbi water spirits, Ife, and so on. If a historical time period were associated with the world, it seems as if it'd be "medieval" / pre-substantial European contact - there are passing references to "white-skinned people" from far away but they don't substantially interact with the story. 

The most notable feature of the novel is that it's very, for lack of a better word, vulgar. To give a sense, the typical summary from reviewers is something like an "African Game of Thrones", and from what I know of ASOIAF, this about checks out. There's, in particular, a lot of sex (including a lot of sexual violence) and gore, and fairly detailed descriptions of both, though James manages to keep it from feeling gratuitous overall. It's vaguely worth noting that it is also nominally queer but moreso in the sense that homosexuality is simply perceived as rather typical (in a rather cynical manner). 

It is noted multiple times that the narrator/main character, Tracker, is quite misogynistic, and this definitely shines through in a lot of places - there is definitely at least a not-so-subtle undercurrent of misogyny throughout the text. This doesn't get particularly jarring overall, though it's very much worth noting. 

Without going into details, the plot really picks up after a short introductory section, when the main plot is revealed to Tracker. It took a bit for me to get to that point, though, which slowed me down quite a bit. The ending is also quite jarring after finishing the "main" plot, although it is extremely sensible and ties the whole plot thematically together. It's pretty baffling there's a sequel, since I don't know how you'd expand on this, but I'll find out hopefully soon-ish I guess.

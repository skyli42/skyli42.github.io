**Format**: Digital (ty Project Gutenberg)

I finally got around to reading another Sherlock Holmes novel after [the last two]({% link _reviews/study_in_scarlet_sign_of_four.md %}). Without spoiling anything (I guess?), I found _The Hound of the Baskervilles_ to be a much more cohesively told story than either of _A Study in Scarlet_ and _The Sign of Four_; overall, the characters feel more developed and the story carries the reader along much more smoothly than in either of the other two novels. In large part, this is because most of the action of the story is told from Watson's POV (as usual), but without Holmes being present at all (supposedly off in London solving some other case). Although the reader is usually left mostly in the dark for the bulk of detective stories, this turns the novel from a pure mystery into more of a thriller mystery type thing, as Watson manages to get himself into many more Incidents than typically would happen with Holmes around. The focus on a select group of characters with a bit more time to breathe, (Barrymores, Stapletons, Henry Baskerville, etc.) also means that each character feels like more than a prop for the mystery to unfold. I particularly enjoyed the repeated intrusions of Dr Mortimer into random bouts of literal skull measuring phrenology, and the clear exasperation of every other character whenever it happened.

_Valley of Fear_ next, I guess. I think its the one of these novels I know the least about so that should be fun.

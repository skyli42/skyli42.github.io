**Author**: Fyodor Dostoevsky

**Format**: Paperback

_The Gambler_ follows Alexei Ivanovich, a tutor to an expatriate Russian family in Germany, as he ragebaits his way into poverty. The patriarch of the family, a general(?), spends his time waiting for his wealthy grandmother to die to inherit so he can marry a local noble woman of some sort. Alexei then spends most of his time genuinely just ragebaiting the General's friends and his love interest, the lady Polina, by making random accusations about Frenchmen and the like. At some point, Alexei impulsively insults a German noble on the "orders" of Polina and gets fired. Then suddenly the grandmother shows up (!!!) and is notably, not dead, and proceeds to go play roulette with Alexei, gets hooked after one winning streak, and loses the rest of her money. Then she leaves and Alexei kinda spirals into nothingness. 

For a book that is nominally about gambling, there's really not that much gambling of interest to hold your attention. I'm sure there's something very meaningful about the impulsivity of Alexei in his interpersonal relationships and a correspondence to his gambling problem etc. but I simply could not bring myself to care about Alexei or anyone else in the story. That said, the few instances where Dostoevksy describes Alexei playing roulette genuinely suck you in with a well-executed sense of dread, but then they end when Alexei / the grandmother lose all their money and you just have to deal with them being wrecks for 10 more chapters.

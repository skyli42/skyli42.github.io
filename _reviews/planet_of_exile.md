**Author**: Ursula K. Le Guin

**Format**: Hardcover

_Planet of Exile_ is the second book in the Hainish cycle, set between _Rocannon's World_ and _City of Illusions_. The novel is set on "Werel", the home-world of Falk (from _City of Illusions_); uniquely, the planet revolves around the sun on a much longer cycle, so individual years on Werel are ~60 Earth-years long (so, e.g., winter will last decades). At the time of the novel, settlers from the Hainish League have been stranded for about 10 full Werel-years since the war (hinted at in _Rocannon's World_) between the Hainish League (comprised of Terrans) and the Shing started; they inhabit a dwindling settlement near the native Tevarans.

The novel centres on the relationship between Rolery, a Tevaran, and Jakob Agat Alterra, one of the leaders of the Terran settlement, who fall in love (? unclear) and eventually sleep together - this causes Rolery to be exiled and exacerbates tensions between the two groups just a barbarian-like tribe, the Gaal, sweep down and begin conquering the region. After the sacking of the Tevaran city, the Terran settlement holds out with the survivors until the Gaal are forced to leave them alone. 

It's possible that it's just because I read the other two books before this, but _Planet of Exile_ definitely feels weaker than _Rocannon's World_ and _City of Illusions_. In large part, I didn't particularly vibe with the interpersonal conflict that is the core of the novel (or Rolery / Alterra's relationship) - it ended up feeling pretty tropey / not particularly novel, and there wasn't much else in the short plot to hold me. It's a fun snapshot at one interaction between Hainish League settlers and the natives of the planets they reach out to, but didn't feel particularly meaningful beyond that (though in the overarching plot, it sets up that the Terrans and Teverans can interbreed to the point of saving both of their species, and sets up the eventual return in _City of Illusions_).
